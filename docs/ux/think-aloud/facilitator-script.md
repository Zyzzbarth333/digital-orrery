# Facilitator script

Words to say are in plain text; notes for you are in *italics*. Read the
welcome as written, so every participant hears the same thing; after that,
talk naturally.

## Before they arrive (5 min)

*Firefox open on a fresh profile at the first task's start link
([README](README.md#running-it-in-firefox-on-the-laptop)), the build's
commit written on the recording sheet, scenario cards in order, consent note
ready, timer at zero.*

## Welcome (5 min)

Thanks for coming. Today I'm testing a solar-system simulator I've been
building. It runs in a web browser and shows the planets where they really
are, and you can launch and fly spacecraft in it.

I'm testing the app, not you. You can't do anything wrong. If something is
confusing, that's useful: it tells me what to fix.

*Go through the [consent note](consent.md). Start recording only if they
ticked the box.*

## Thinking aloud: a practice (3 min)

As you work, I'd like you to say out loud whatever you're thinking: what
you're looking at, what you expect to happen, what surprises you. It feels
odd at first, so let's practise. Look around this room, and tell me out
loud how you'd find out what time it is.

*Praise the talking, not the answer: "That's exactly the kind of thing."*

I won't help much while you work, because I want to see how the app does
on its own. If you get stuck, try what you'd try at home. If you'd give
up, say so: that's a fine result.

## The tasks (about 25 min)

*For each task, in order:*

1. *Load the task's start link and wait until the view has settled.*
2. Here is the next task. *Hand over the card, or read it aloud.* Please
   read it out loud, then start whenever you're ready.
3. *Start the timer when they first touch the mouse or keyboard. Stop it
   when they say they're done, or at the task's time limit.*
4. *Watch and write: where they look first, wrong turns (each one is an
   error), what they say, and the words they use for things.*
5. *After the task:* On a scale of 1 to 7, where 1 is very difficult and 7
   is very easy, how easy or difficult was that task? *(The SEQ: write the
   number on the sheet.)*
6. *If they did not finish:* Thanks; let's move on. *(Don't show the
   answer yet: it can change how they do the next task. Show them at the
   end if they want.)*

**Keeping them talking.** When they go quiet for more than about ten
seconds, use one of these, and nothing more leading:

- What are you thinking now?
- What are you looking for?
- What did you expect to happen there?

**When to help.** Only when they're stuck: no progress for about a minute,
or they ask twice. Help in steps, and write down each one on the sheet:

1. What would you try next?
2. Is there anywhere on the screen you haven't looked yet?
3. *Point to the area (for example the header) without naming the
   control.*
4. *Name the control.* That counts as "success with help".

**Don't** explain the app, name controls before the task is over, agree or
disagree with what they say, or say "good" when they find something (say
"thanks" instead).

## After the tasks (10 min)

*Hand over the [SUS questions](recording-sheet.md#system-usability-scale-sus)
and let them fill it in on their own.*

Then ask, and write the answers down in their words:

1. What was the hardest part?
2. Was there anything you expected the app to do that it didn't?
3. In one sentence, how would you describe this app to a friend?
4. If you could change one thing, what would it be?
5. What are the three things someone needs to know to use it well?

*Stop the recording.* Thank you, that was really useful. *Show them any
task they didn't finish, if they'd like to see it.*

## Straight after (10 min)

*While it's fresh: finish the recording sheet, add the problems you saw to
the round's list, and note anything to change before the next participant
(RITE: fix the obvious things between sessions, and note what changed).*
