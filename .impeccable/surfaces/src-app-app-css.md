# Orrery console (the main screen)

Scope: the panels over the 3D scene (time band, body rail, data plate, scene markers). Mode: Operate.
Audience and job: the engineer reads exact values and drives time and focus (keyboard included); a portfolio visitor grasps "real solar system, real numbers" in seconds, on a phone too.
Constraints: Australian English; original retro-futurist mission-console feel, no film references; standard web controls; works at 390 px.

## Direction contract

seed: none (pinned)

THESIS: A flight console whose every number shows where it came from. Refuses the floating glass cards of space-viz apps.

OWN-WORLD: Warm graphite console ground (solid, square-cornered, 1px warm hairlines), warm off-white text, one phosphor-amber accent for selection, focus and the active state; red and amber only as state. B612 for labels, B612 Mono with tabular figures for every number and the clock (Airbus cockpit faces).

STORY: The 3D sky is the work area; a full-width time band, a full-height body rail and a ruled data plate frame it like a console around a viewport.

FIRST VIEWPORT: The true-scale sky with the UTC clock running in the band, the Sun focused, and the data plate's provenance column visible at once.

SIGNATURE: The provenance column: each readout in the data plate carries its source tag (DE442, NAIF 599 sat441, NASA NSSDCA, IAU) beside the value.

RISK: Density can read cramped on a phone; the rail becomes a horizontal strip and the plate collapses below 720 px.
